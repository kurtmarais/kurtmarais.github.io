abstract: |
  This research explores how affective information — particularly sentiment related to depression — spreads through online social networks, and what that means beyond the underlying computational study itself. Framed by emotional contagion and infodemiology, the work identifies a phenomenon of *emotional reinforcement*: socially connected individuals experience intensified, longer-lasting shared emotion when they hold the same diagnosis and emotional state, with particular relevance for people experiencing depression.

  The research has since informed conversations with social media users, platform developers, mental health practitioners, and policymakers — from advocating for user-controlled tools that reduce algorithmic influence, to informing the development of a depression-specific sentiment lexicon toward a self-evaluation tool for patients and clinicians. The work has also reached a wider public audience through national and local media coverage.
