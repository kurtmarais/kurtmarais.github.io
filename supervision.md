### Completed supervision

**Ntswanano Maswanganye** · BComHons (Operations Research) · 2025<br>
Project title: *Feature engineering for predicting movie success*
<details class="supervision-abstract"><summary>Abstract</summary>
The global film industry represents a multi-billion-dollar sector in which production decisions are often made under high uncertainty. Despite the availability of large volumes of data on film performance, accurately predicting a movie’s success prior to release remains a complex and multidimensional challenge. This research investigates how feature engineering, in conjunction with advanced machine learning algorithms, can improve the predictive modelling of movie success. Success is defined through both financial performance, measured by worldwide gross revenue, and critical reception, measured by meta scores from IMDb and Rotten Tomatoes. Data were compiled from publicly available sources, including Kaggle, IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes, and Letterboxd, and subjected to extensive preprocessing procedures involving data cleaning, integration, and transformation. Several derived variables were introduced through feature engineering. Release weekend popularity, marketing efficiency, and annualised revenue to capture the temporal and contextual dimensions of film performance. Feature selection was performed using Pearson correlation and mutual information to identify the most informative predictors while mitigating multicollinearity. Four regression-based machine learning algorithms, namely random forest, light gradient boosting machine (LightGBM), AdaBoost, and linear regression were implemented and optimised through hyperparameter tuning and 10-fold cross-validation. Model performance was assessed using mean squared error, mean absolute error, and the coefficient of determination. The results indicate that feature engineering substantially enhances predictive performance across all models, with ensemble methods random forest and LightGBM consistently outperforming simpler linear models. Furthermore, the analysis of feature importance revealed that variables related to production budget, marketing efficiency, and opening weekend gross revenue were the most influential determinants of film success. This research demonstrates that combining feature engineering with supervised machine learning yields a powerful, interpretable approach to predicting movie success. The outcomes of the study add to the growing literature at the intersection of data science and entertainment economics. Moreover, the insights gained have practical relevance for producers, investors, and marketing teams, who can leverage data-driven models to optimise decision-making and enhance the financial viability of film production and distribution.

**Avaniya Pillay** · BComHons (Operations Research) · 2025<br>
Project title: *Feature engineering for song success prediction*
<details class="supervision-abstract"><summary>Abstract</summary>
The introduction of music streaming platforms has led to an increase in music consumption across the globe. As a result, various stakeholders in the music industry are interested in identifying the elements of a song that result in more streams on music streaming platforms, ultimately increasing song popularity. This study identifies the features of a song that contribute to its success on music streaming platforms using feature engineering and machine learning. A Spotify dataset containing various song features of songs from 1950 to 2020 was used, which was obtained from Kaggle. Data cleaning and preprocessing were applied to the dataset. Artist popularity scores were extracted using the Spotify API. Six new song features were engineered. Once all features were engineered, correlation analysis was conducted to remove multicollinear features. Five tree-based ensemble models, namely bagging, random forests, gradient boosting, extreme gradient boosting and light gradient boosting, were constructed to predict song popularity scores. Various model hyperparameters were tested for each model using five-fold cross-validation to determine the optimal model configurations. The results showed that extreme gradient boosting yielded consistently low RMSE, MAE and high R<sup>2</sup>sup> scores with an RMSE of 0.1598, MAE of 0.1239 and R<sup>2</sup>sup> of 23.10%. All models yielded low R<sup>2</sup>sup> scores, suggesting that there were features not accounted for that explain the variation in song popularity scores. This was due to the simplifying assumptions of the model, such that external factors such as social media, were not considered. All models were also tested with and without feature engineering. Feature engineering improved model accuracy and was particularly beneficial to the R<sup>2</sup>sup> scores of the random forest, gradient boosting, extreme gradient boosting and light gradient boosting models. Feature importance analysis was also conducted, highlighting that the most significant features of song popularity are the popularity of the main artist and the wordiness of a song. Pop, rock and EDM songs were more popular than other genres. Potential avenues of future research include using more representative song and artist popularity scores, investigating the lyrical content of a song by sections and incorporating virality effects as a feature.

**Tineke Wilcocks** · BDatSci (Analytics and Optimisation) · 2025<br>
Project title: *Enhancing fashion image classification using deep learning and targeted dataset segmentation*
<details class="supervision-abstract"><summary>Abstract</summary>
The fashion industry is one of the largest categories in the global e-commerce industry and demands integration advanced technology more than ever before. This research addresses the fashion image classification problem by way of artificial intelligence techniques and compares the power of simplicity to the performance of complex operations. The methodology consists of several fundamental steps. Data preprocessing was performed, such as feature selection, the handling of missing values, class groupings and the removal of duplicate features. Additionally, the dataset was categorised into five different subsets to be used for model training and image classification to determine if creating subsets for unique genders or item subcategories would improve the models’ performance. A simple and a complex implementation of the convolutional neural network architectures were proposed for the classification of eleven multi-class outputs, with the residual network consisting of fifty layers as the baseline. The models were trained on each of the subsets to determine the most beneficial split of the dataset for model performance. Model performance was evaluated based on accuracy, F1-scores, and precision scores, as well as AUC-ROC plots for the binary outputs of the models. The models were evaluated for each attribute according to each subset of the full dataset. The results indicated that the simple implementation of the convolutional neural network outperformed its counterparts on the basis of stability, scalability, and performance. Furthermore, the division of the dataset into female and male subsets reflected the most favourable results. Future research directions may consider the compilation of more comprehensive datasets, or tuning the hyperparameters regarding the batch size and number of epochs used in training of the convolutional neural network implementations to improve classification performance.
